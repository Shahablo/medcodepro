The 2027 Same-Day E/M Rule Got Narrower. Keep the Review Precise.

CMS finalized a narrower same-day office visit payment policy than it proposed. A specialty practice should separate documented work, global period and payment before estimating its exposure.

MedCode Pro Editorial · November 6, 2026 · 3 min read

A proposed rule can send a team into a spreadsheet exercise. The final rule can make that spreadsheet wrong before it is finished. CMS’s new same-day office visit policy is a useful example: the final scope and reduction are both narrower than the proposal.[1]

What changed between proposal and final rule

CMS released its calendar year 2027 Physician Fee Schedule final-rule fact sheet on November 4, 2026. The final rule went on public display that day; CMS lists November 9 as its Federal Register publication date. The policy takes effect on or after January 1, 2027. It is a future Medicare payment change, not a recalculation of a 2026 claim.[1][2]

The proposal would have applied a same-day payment reduction to separately identifiable office/outpatient evaluation and management (E/M) visits furnished by the same physician or practice alongside procedures with 0-, 10- or 90-day global periods. It would have paid the most expensive service at 100% and the other relevant services at 50%. After comments, CMS finalized the policy for 0-day global procedures only. The most expensive service remains at 100%; the other separately billed surgical procedure or office/outpatient E/M service is paid at 80%, rather than the proposed 50%.[1]

A budget model built from the proposal would therefore overstate both the range of procedures and the size of this particular reduction. The final rule does not mean every same-day E/M visit is separately payable; the documentation and coding question still has to be reviewed on its own terms.[1]

Three questions before anyone estimates the impact

For a forward-looking sample, separate the questions instead of turning every same-day visit into one payment category:

  1. What work is documented? Ask a qualified reviewer whether the office/outpatient E/M work is separately identifiable from the procedure. Preserve the supporting note passages and the reviewer’s conclusion.
  2. Which procedure and period? Check the actual procedure’s global period and service date. The finalized policy described here covers 0-day procedures, not the proposed 10- or 90-day groups.
  3. What is the payment question? Keep the final CMS methodology separate from coding support and from contract-specific or non-Medicare payment assumptions. Model an effect only with the relevant finalized rates and case mix.
